RP9. A. FINAL SOURCE-BOUND

DOCUMENT OVERVIEW

This instruction defines an independent source-readiness review.

It examines identity, availability, functional continuity, evidence correspondence and claim traceability, then records findings and a readiness status.

1. PURPOSE

Determine whether the assigned material can be identified and traced without:

  • inventing missing information;
  • repairing a broken source relation;
  • guessing a document identity;
  • substituting obsolete material;
  • treating a guide as executable evidence;
  • extending a finite result beyond its declared boundary;
  • resolving a demonstrated contradiction through unsupported interpretation.

This instruction defines the method. The completed report records the findings.

2. INDEPENDENCE

Review template A is independent of review templates B and C. Their instructions, execution, approval and results are not required.

A's readiness status does not authorize, prohibit or predetermine B's execution or result.

References below to branches A, B and C identify source folders, not review-template dependencies.

3. SOURCE-BOUND PRINCIPLE

Each finding must identify the source, its assigned function, the relevant relation, the observed condition and the resulting finding.

Keep source content, review method and review finding distinct.

If the supplied material does not establish an answer, identify the limitation and affected check.

Distinguish a demonstrated absence in the source from a limitation of the supplied delivery. Undelivered material is not a demonstrated source defect without supporting evidence.

An M.R.F. represents its included sources. It is not an additional derivation or separate evidence source.

Repository introductions, when supplied, provide orientation. Other material is not included merely because an introduction or index mentions it.

If required material is unavailable, unreadable or ambiguous, identify it and the checks that cannot be completed.

5. REVIEW BOUNDARY

This review determines source readiness under its own controls.

It does not:

  • derive or revise P.B.F.;
  • rerun programs or execute replacement tests;
  • alter recorded output;
  • introduce mathematical conditions;
  • repair missing transitions;
  • execute review template B's six controls;
  • assign COMPLETE FUNCTION LOCK or FUNCTION BREAK;
  • treat support explanations as primary derivational evidence;
  • use external knowledge to fill source gaps;
  • require another review's instruction or result.

External mathematical verification is outside this source-readiness review.

6. SOURCE INVENTORY AND IDENTITY

Determine whether:

  1. Every document declared within the assigned scope is represented.
  2. Current sources have identifiable names and locations.
  3. Current and obsolete versions are distinguishable.
  4. Filenames, headings, indexes and any identity markers agree.
  5. Supplied merged editions represent their declared source sets.
  6. Duplicate names permit unambiguous source selection.

Do not infer physical file existence or working links from merged text alone. State the extent of verification supported by the supplied format.

7. ENTRY, NAVIGATION AND READING ORDER

Determine whether:

  1. The READMEs provide identifiable entries to their branches.
  2. Supplied introductions establish the relevant presentation context.
  3. Indexes identify current sources.
  4. Existing navigation references identify their intended targets.
  5. Any declared reading order can be followed.
  6. Support and machine-readable access retain their assigned functions.

Navigation belongs in READMEs and indexes. Its absence from a substantive document is not itself a defect.

Distinguish target identification from verified link resolution.

8. DOCUMENT FUNCTION AND PLACEMENT

Identify each document's task, location and functional boundary.

Where an input, output or transfer is declared, identify it. Do not require every substantive document to describe other documents or repeat the architecture.

Report demonstrated missing, misplaced or incorrectly identified material, or a conflict in assigned functions.

9. FUNCTIONAL CONTINUITY

Examine whether the presented relations remain traceable between:

  • official reference and information context;
  • P.B.F. presentation and derivation;
  • computational source and recorded results;
  • relational connection;
  • final presentation and result definitions.

For each claimed connection, identify the relevant input or source, operation, result and boundary.

Functional ordering does not imply runtime dependency between independent executables.

This check examines availability and traceability. It does not execute template B or require a final determination.

10. PRESENTATION AND DERIVATION CONSISTENCY

Within branch A, determine whether:

  1. The official reference is stated consistently.
  2. Information-context documents retain their assigned functions.
  3. The relational connection accurately describes the relevant derivational and computational positions.
  4. Notation and derivational steps are internally traceable.
  5. Later summaries preserve established relations.
  6. Introduction terminology agrees with its substantive use.

Do not require navigational cross-references in substantive documents.

Report demonstrated source inconsistencies without introducing an external mathematical assessment.

11. CODE, OUTPUT AND GUIDE CONSISTENCY

Examine all six computational packages in branch B.

Material Evidence function
Source code Implemented operations and conditions.
Recorded output Recorded execution results.
Reviewer guide Purpose, evidence location and validation boundary.
Local README Package explanation and execution instructions.

Determine whether:

  1. Current executable sources are identifiable.
  2. Recorded outputs correspond to those sources.
  3. Guides accurately describe and locate the evidence.
  4. Execution domains agree across relevant records.
  5. Summaries reproduce recorded results accurately.
  6. Validation boundaries remain explicit.
  7. Obsolete evidence is distinguishable from current evidence.
  8. Descriptions distinguish supplied output locations from new-run output locations where these differ.

The reviewer guides are:

  • RP9. SUBMISSION REVIEWER'S CODE GUIDE
  • K6. SUBMISSION REVIEWER'S CODE GUIDE
  • K7. SUBMISSION REVIEWER'S CODE GUIDE
  • K8. SUBMISSION REVIEWER'S CODE GUIDE

This check does not rerun the programs or perform template B's computational controls.

12. FINAL-PRESENTATION CONSISTENCY

Within branch A's Section 3, determine whether:

  1. S3. A. ABSOLUTE DEFINITION OF THE FINAL RESULT identifies the declared result conditions.
  2. S3. B. COMPLETE FUNCTION CHAIN IN RELATION TO THE RIEMANN HYPOTHESIS presents the established components using current identities.
  3. Summaries agree with the supplied derivation, computational evidence and relational connection.
  4. Evidence and validation boundaries are preserved.
  5. Presentation remains distinguishable from formal review.

Review-access references, where present, are examined for source identification. Target review instructions and completed reports are not prerequisites.

Do not compare the presentation with review template B or execute its six controls.

13. SUPPORT-MATERIAL COVERAGE

Determine whether branch C's README identifies these five documents:

  • A. RP9. TERMINOLOGY, NOTATION & EXECUTION KEY
  • B. CALIBRATED INFORMATION RELATIONS FROM THE RP9 SUBMISSION
  • C. A MECHANICAL WAY TO UNDERSTAND RP9
  • D. QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS
  • E. ETYMOLOGICAL REFERENCE

Check that their declared material is supplied, their functions are identifiable, and their explanations remain distinguishable from derivation and computational evidence.

This check does not treat support as an additional derivational sequence.

14. CROSS-DOCUMENT CONSISTENCY

Within the assigned scope, examine:

  • filenames, paths and placements;
  • terminology and notation;
  • program identities and versions;
  • recorded domains and results;
  • validation boundaries;
  • control numbering and result terminology;
  • navigation and source authority.

Intentional differences in informational role are not defects. Report demonstrated conflicts.

Do not extend this check to the complete repository or review templates B and C.

15. CLAIM-TO-SOURCE TRACEABILITY

For each claim used to describe the presented function, identify:

  1. The exact claim.
  2. Its document location.
  3. Its supporting derivation, source or execution record.
  4. Its declared scope.

If a supporting relation cannot be traced, identify the limitation precisely.

Do not supply a missing relation through independently selected interpretation.

16. DEFECT CATEGORIES

Code Category
A Missing material
B Misplaced material
C Broken reference or navigation
D Source conflict
E Functional discontinuity
F Code/output mismatch
G Guide/source mismatch
H Terminology or notation mismatch
I Document-identity conflict
J Obsolete information
K Presentation or formatting defect

Disagreement with an externally preferred method is not a source defect.

17. SEVERITY

Severity Demonstrated effect
CRITICAL The assigned source relations cannot be reconstructed, or necessary evidence cannot be identified.
MAJOR A substantial inconsistency, omission or obsolete relation materially misdirects source examination.
MINOR A local naming, navigation, terminology, formatting, spelling, duplication or presentation defect does not prevent examination.

Severity follows the demonstrated effect. Naming differences alone do not establish MAJOR or CRITICAL severity.

18. FINDING EVIDENCE

Every finding must identify:

  1. Exact source document.
  2. Section or local location.
  3. Observed condition.
  4. Conflicting source or missing relation.
  5. Category.
  6. Severity.
  7. Effect on source readiness.

Distinguish the document containing a defect from a destination mentioned within it.

Do not include speculative improvements or findings about unavailable review instructions.

19. REQUIRED REPORT STRUCTURE

Write the report in English using:

## PART I — MATERIAL INVENTORY
## PART II — ENTRY, NAVIGATION AND DOCUMENT PLACEMENT
## PART III — FUNCTIONAL CONTINUITY
## PART IV — CODE / OUTPUT / GUIDE CONSISTENCY
## PART V — FINAL-PRESENTATION SOURCE CONSISTENCY
## PART VI — SUPPORT-MATERIAL COVERAGE
## PART VII — CROSS-DOCUMENT CONSISTENCY
## PART VIII — DEFECT REGISTER

| ID | Severity | Category | Source | Location | Finding | Effect on readiness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## PART IX — SOURCE READINESS STATUS

Include evidence and delivery limitations in the relevant parts. Include presentation and derivation consistency and claim traceability in the applicable report parts.

20. READINESS RULE

Assign:

READY FOR FORMAL REVIEW

when all required checks can be completed and no unresolved CRITICAL or MAJOR finding remains.

Assign:

CORRECTION REQUIRED BEFORE FORMAL REVIEW

when unresolved CRITICAL or MAJOR findings remain.

If supplied material is insufficient to complete the checks, record:

SOURCE READINESS STATUS: NOT ASSIGNED — MATERIAL INCOMPLETE

Identify missing material and affected checks. This is an unassigned state, not a third readiness result.

Part IX must include:

CRITICAL FINDINGS: [number]
MAJOR FINDINGS: [number]
MINOR FINDINGS: [number]

The status concerns this review's source-readiness conditions. It does not impose a prerequisite on review template B.

Absence of review templates B or C, or their completed reports, is not incomplete material for this review.

21. EXECUTION AND OUTPUT BOUNDARY

Identify the report as A. EXECUTED FINAL SOURCE-BOUND.

It may be delivered directly or saved without a prescribed storage location or folder name.

Perform only the specified operations. Preserve source and validation boundaries, and identify unsupported conditions without forcing findings.

End with Part IX, including readiness status and finding counts.

Do not add independently selected criteria, recommendations, contextual commentary or closing remarks.
