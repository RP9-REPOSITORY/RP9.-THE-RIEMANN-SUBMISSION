D. QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS

DOCUMENT OVERVIEW

This document answers twelve interpretative questions about P.B.F., positional genealogy, external comparison, representation and claim scope.

The answers clarify the presented material without introducing new derivations, computational evidence or review conditions.

1. IS P.B.F. A NUMERICAL FORMULA?

No. P.B.F. is defined as a primary binary function, expressed through the statement:

1 VALUE=2 PRIMARY FUNCTIONS.

Fractions and other numerical expressions represent the positions and relations established through its operations.

P.B.F. can therefore be represented numerically without its functional definition being reduced to an isolated formula.

2. DOES P.B.F. GENERATE THE SEQUENCE OR DESCRIBE AN EXISTING PATTERN?

Within the presented definition, its operations establish the relational structure.

Starting from a declared whole 𝐼, binary division and continued halving establish equal positions, then an operationally differentiated state. Regrouping expresses the resulting complementary relation.

The rational expressions describe the outcomes of those operations. They are not a finished external pattern supplied to the function.

3. DOES K7 INTRODUCE A NEW PRIMARY FUNCTION?

No. Its assigned role is positional execution of the presented P.B.F. function.

K7 has a different computational task from K.6. That distinction does not imply a new primary function or an operator applied to K.6 output.

K7 generates its observation field and genealogical classifications internally. It does not require K.6 output as runtime input.

4. HOW DOES K7 EXPRESS THE PRESENTED FUNCTION POSITIONALLY?

Through established references, sequential continuation, positional coincidence and closure.

An active reference advances its endpoints through repeated addition. Coincidence with an observation position determines whether that position has been reproduced.

The intended correspondence is:

Relational concept Positional execution
Established position Initial or internally generated reference.
Continued operation Sequential endpoint advancement.
Repetition Repeated addition of the reference.
Reproduction Earlier genealogy reaches a later observation position.
Positional distinction Reached and unreached observations are classified separately.
Closure An unreached observation remains a new closure.
Continued genealogy Eligible closures participate as references according to the implementation.

The correspondence concerns operations and their assigned meanings, rather than identical wording between conceptual explanation and code.

5. DOES K7 USE AN EXTERNAL PRIME LIST TO GENERATE ITS INTERNAL RESULT?

No. The internal generator uses its locked observation field and internally generated references.

Candidate classification does not use:

  • primality tests;
  • factor searches;
  • divisibility queries for candidate classification;
  • modulo classification during candidate evaluation;
  • a 𝑝2 start rule;
  • an externally supplied list of later prime positions.

The conventional prime reference is generated separately for comparison. Its data is not passed into the internal generator.

6. WHAT DISTINGUISHES REPRODUCED FROM NEW CLOSURE?

Classification Meaning
REPRODUCED An observation position reached by preceding genealogy.
NEW CLOSURE An observation position not reached by preceding genealogy.

These are internal genealogical classifications, not labels imported from the external reference.

A new closure does not terminate the process. It can participate in continued genealogy according to the implemented reference mechanism.

7. DOES A DIFFERENCE BETWEEN CONCEPTUAL LANGUAGE AND CODE IMPLY INCOMPLETE CORRESPONDENCE?

No. Different forms of expression do not by themselves establish a functional gap.

The presented correspondence concerns the observation field, references, endpoint continuation, classification and closure. Its completeness depends on whether the implementation preserves the operations required by its assigned task.

An explanation of correspondence is not itself proof of that correspondence. The definitions and executable operations must support it.

Segmented execution does not interrupt the intended continuity: active endpoint states are retained across memory boundaries.

8. WHAT DOES THE EXTERNAL PRIME REFERENCE CHECK?

It checks whether the internally generated closure positions coincide with an independently constructed conventional prime-reference field.

The comparison detects:

  • internally marked positions absent from the reference;
  • reference positions absent from the internal result;
  • positional disagreement and its first location.

The conventional base reference is prepared before segment execution. Each comparison bitmap is constructed after its corresponding internal segment is complete.

The recorded execution through 100 000 000 000 reports exact positional agreement, zero false positives, zero false negatives, no first deviation and global PASS.

Those results concern the executed domain. Matching totals alone would not establish positional agreement.

9. DOES BASE 10 CREATE THE P.B.F. RELATIONS?

No. It represents already established rational states.

Decimal encoding makes those values expressible in Base 10. Exact reconstruction checks that the representation preserves them.

The use of a decimal representation does not make the decimal system the generative origin of the represented relation.

10. WHAT DOES K8. C CONTROL?

It checks finite Base-10 encoding and exact rational reconstruction of five defined states:

Source state Decimal representation
12 0.5
34 0.75
14 0.25
18 0.125
1 1

It also checks representation identity across equivalent binary-scale expressions, expected values and exact complementary closure.

The recorded execution reports eight passed projection levels, cross-level identity PASS and BASE-10 PROJECTION LOCK PASS.

The checks use exact rational arithmetic. Floating-point tolerance does not determine the result.

K8. C tests preservation of the source states; it does not derive their conceptual origin or the Base-10 number system.

11. IS THE PROPOSED RESOLUTION CONTAINED IN ONE ISOLATED EQUATION?

No. The proposed resolution depends on the supporting derivation, executed controls, recorded evidence and stated relational connection.

An isolated numerical equality omits the reasoning needed to connect those results to the official Riemann statement.

Likewise, locating or explaining the proposed resolution does not validate it. Presentation and evaluation remain distinct.

The connection to the official problem must be supported by the material, rather than inferred from a shared value or similar wording.

12. MUST RP9 ANSWER EVERY QUESTION ABOUT NUMBERS, GEOMETRY AND INFINITY?

No. It must provide the information necessary to support its stated claims.

Additional applications, geometric interpretations and representational consequences are not automatically requirements merely because they are related.

A question remains relevant when it exposes a necessary missing definition, derivation, computational condition or logical connection.

Minimalism does not excuse such omissions. In particular, a claim about the full Riemann Hypothesis requires support for its universal scope; a finite execution alone does not establish that scope.
